The business in plain English

1. Business model

GitLab sells one platform for the whole software lifecycle: source code, CI/CD pipelines, security scanning, deployment and, increasingly, AI agents that work inside that flow (GitLab Duo). Customers pay per seat per year, either self-managed or on GitLab.com/GitLab Dedicated. Pricing tiers (Free, Premium, Ultimate) push customers up to Ultimate for security and compliance [P1].

Q2 FY2027 revenue was $286.3M, up 21%, beating the $272–274M guide by ~5% [P1] [N1]. Current remaining performance obligations rose 20% to $744.7M. FY2027 guidance: $1.129–1.133B revenue, $148–152M non-GAAP operating income [P1].

Fiscal year ends 31 January. In June 2026 GitLab cut ~350 roles (~14%) and exited 22 countries, reinvesting savings into R&D and AI [N1].

2. Moat

The moat is workflow lock-in. Once a company's repositories, pipelines, security policies and audit trail run on GitLab, moving to another platform means rebuilding years of CI/CD configuration. Regulated customers (banks, government, defence) value the self-managed and Dedicated options, which GitHub serves less well [P1].

  • GitHub (Microsoft) is the default for developers and owns Copilot, the leading AI coding assistant.
  • AI-native coding tools compete for the same developer budget and could make the platform less central if agents work outside it.
  • Atlassian, JFrog and CI/CD point tools compete in parts of the stack.

The bear case in spring 2026 was that AI agents would shrink seat counts. Q2 eased that worry: net retention rose and bookings hit a record [N4].

3. Catalysts, next 12 months

  • Q3 FY2027 results, ~1 Dec 2026 (estimated, not yet confirmed). Guide: revenue $281–283M, non-GAAP operating income $35–37M, EPS $0.19–0.20 [P1]. Note the guide is below Q2's actual $286.3M; GitLab beat its last two revenue guides by ~4–5% [N1] [P1].
  • FY2028 guidance with Q4 results, ~early Mar 2027.

  • AI products: GitLab Orbit (public beta), agentic automation in 19.x, the Governed Software Factory [N6] [N3]. Real but undated.
  • Takeover angle: Datadog was reported in Oct 2025 to be weighing an offer above $60/share [N2]. Nothing confirmed since; don't trade on it.

4. Asymmetry

Floor: $1.26B net cash (~13% of the ~$9.7B market cap), ~$150M of buybacks per half year, and recurring subscription revenue [P1] [N3].

Ceiling: the stock has tripled from a $18.73 low and closed at $57.97 on 28 Oct, above the ~$56 average analyst target (28 analysts) [N3]. That's ~67× FY2027 non-GAAP EPS and ~7.4× EV/revenue. Upside from here needs more beat-and-raise quarters, not a re-rating.

Stronger side: downside. The fundamentals improved, but the price already reflects that; a merely in-line Q3 would hurt more than a beat helps.

5. Bull and bear

Bull: AI writes more code, and every line still needs pipelines, security scans and governance, which GitLab sells. Net new ARR +40%, net retention rising to 117%, a leaner cost base after the restructuring, net cash and steady buybacks. Guidance has been conservative twice in a row.

Bear: ~67× forward non-GAAP earnings for 21% growth, while GAAP is still loss-making and stock compensation runs ~$300M a year. GitHub plus Copilot is the bigger platform. After tripling, the stock trades above the average analyst target.
