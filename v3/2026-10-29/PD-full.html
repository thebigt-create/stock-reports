The business in plain English

1. Business model

PagerDuty sells software that wakes up the right engineer when a digital service breaks, and then coordinates the fix: alerting, on-call schedules, incident response, status pages and automation. It is sold as a subscription per user, with a newer usage-based Operations Cloud (being renamed PD Reliability Platform) that adds AI agents to detect and investigate outages [P1] [P2].

Q2 FY2027 revenue was $124.4M, up 0.8%. ARR passed $501M. FY2027 guidance: $491.5–496.5M revenue (about flat), 25–26% non-GAAP operating margin, non-GAAP EPS $1.33–1.37 [P1].

Fiscal year ends 31 January. 15,506 paid customers, 884 with ARR above $100K [P1].

2. Moat

The moat is workflow lock-in. PagerDuty sits in the middle of a company's monitoring tools and on-call rotas, with hundreds of integrations; ripping it out during live operations is risky. Brand recognition among engineers is strong [P1].

  • ServiceNow and Atlassian bundle incident management into IT service suites.
  • Datadog bundles on-call into monitoring.
  • Newer AI-native incident tools compete for the next generation of buyers.

What PagerDuty lacks: growth. Net retention below 100% means the installed base is shrinking slightly before new sales [P1].

3. Catalysts, next 12 months

  • Q3 FY2027 results, ~24 Nov 2026 (Yahoo date). Guide: revenue $123–125M, non-GAAP EPS $0.34–0.36 [P1].
  • PD Reliability Platform GA this quarter, with usage-based pricing and an AI SRE agent [P2].
  • FY2028 guidance with Q4 results, ~mid-March 2027. The first full year under the new CEO.
  • Possible sale. PD explored a sale with Qatalyst in 2025 [N1]; no deal followed. Undated.
  • Negative watch: net retention. Below 98% would mean the AI products are not stopping the leak.

4. Asymmetry

Floor: ~$117M TTM free cash flow on a ~$1.4B market cap, ~13× FY2027 non-GAAP earnings, and 25%+ operating margins after the cost cut [P1]. A buyer (private equity or strategic) would likely pay up for that cash flow.

Ceiling: a takeover premium, or ARR growth turning back up with the AI platform.

Stronger side: the downside, near term. The stock is at a 52-week high of ~$17.5, well above the 200-day average (~$10.8) and above every average analyst target (~$12.6–12.9) [N2] [Y3], on a business that is not growing.

5. Bull and bear

Bull: a cheap, cash-generating, well-known product under a new CEO. Margins are rising fast, retention is stabilising, and the AI platform could restart growth. If it doesn't, someone may buy the company.

Bear: revenue is flat and customers are spending less each year. Bigger platforms bundle the same function for free or cheap. The stock has already re-rated ~60% on margins, not growth.
