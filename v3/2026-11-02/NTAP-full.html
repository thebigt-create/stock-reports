The business in plain English

1. Business model

NetApp sells enterprise data storage: the systems that hold a company's files, databases and now AI training and inference data, plus the ONTAP software that runs them. Customers buy hardware with multi-year support contracts, or subscribe through Keystone. A smaller Public Cloud business runs NetApp storage inside AWS, Azure and Google Cloud [P3].

Q1 FY2027 revenue was a record $2.03B, up 30% YoY, and FY2027 guidance was raised to $7.975–8.225B, about +17% at the midpoint [P1]. Before mid-2026 the business grew low single digits.

Two segments: Hybrid Cloud (~90%) and Public Cloud (~10%). Most sales go through distributors and resellers; two of them took 43% of FY2026 revenue [P5].

Revenue by segment, Q1 FY2027 (quarter ended 31 Jul 2026, press release):

Hybrid Cloud $1,819M · 90% (of which all-flash arrays ~$1.31B)Public Cloud $206M · 10%

2. Moat

The moat is ONTAP: one storage operating system across on-premises systems and all three big clouds, with decades of enterprise data, backup and ransomware-recovery workflows built on it. Ripping out storage that holds a company's core data is slow and risky, so the installed base renews. Native integrations with AWS, Azure and Google Cloud are hard for newer rivals to copy [P3].

  • Pure Storage is the all-flash specialist and has been growing faster for years [N1].
  • Dell, HPE and IBM sell full-stack hardware; the hyperscalers offer their own cloud storage [P5].

What NetApp lacks: control of its key input. It buys NAND from a few suppliers and has to pass on price spikes, which compresses product margins [P5].

3. Catalysts, next 12 months

  • Q2 FY2027 results, 1 Dec 2026. Guide: revenue $2.025–2.175B, non-GAAP EPS $2.54–2.64 [P1].
  • Q3 FY2027 results, ~late Feb 2027.
  • FY2028 guidance with Q4 results, ~late May 2027. This shows whether the AI step-up lasts.
  • New products from Insight 2026 (29 Sep–1 Oct) and the DataPelago and JetStream acquisitions [N2].
  • Negative watch: NAND prices. A turn in the memory cycle would cut both the price pass-through and the AI-driven urgency.

4. Asymmetry

Floor: net cash of ~$1.0B, ~$1.65B TTM free cash flow, a $0.52 quarterly dividend and steady buybacks. A recurring support base keeps revenue from collapsing in a downturn.

Ceiling: if AI data infrastructure keeps flash demand high, FY2027 non-GAAP EPS of ~$9.9 grows into FY2028. But the mean analyst target (~$212, 16 analysts) already sits below the 23 Oct close of $215 [Y3].

Stronger side: the floor. At ~21.5× forward earnings the market has already re-rated a 3%-grower into a growth stock; more upside needs proof that growth stays above ~15% without NAND price help.

5. Bull and bear

Bull: AI has turned a sleepy storage vendor into a growth story. Enterprises moving AI from pilots to production need fast flash storage next to their data, NetApp already holds that data, and guidance was raised by $650M in one quarter. Cash returns stay generous.

Bear: a chunk of the growth is an extra week and higher NAND prices passed through to customers. When the memory cycle turns, revenue and margins can fall back toward the old low-single-digit trend, and the stock has already doubled to price in the good part.

Verdict · growth, 1–3 years · written 26 Oct 2026
Watch

The turnaround is real: revenue +30%, guidance raised hard, net cash and big buybacks. But this was a 3%-growth company six months ago, part of the jump is an extra week and NAND price pass-through, and the stock already trades above the average analyst target. For a 1–3 year growth investor the open question is durability, not quality.

Dominant risk: a memory-cycle turn. Falling NAND prices would shrink the price-driven part of revenue just as AI urgency fades, and the growth multiple would compress with it.
